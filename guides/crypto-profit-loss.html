Orbit / Profit and loss
Orbit product guide · Portfolio accounting

Crypto profit and loss starts with purchase history.

A wallet balance tells you how much you hold. To calculate profit, you also need to know what you paid, what you sold, and which fees belong to those trades.

How Orbit uses weighted-average cost

For recorded trades, Orbit uses weighted-average purchase cost in USD. Purchase fees increase acquisition cost. A sale removes the corresponding quantity at its average cost; sale fees reduce net proceeds.

A worked example

Suppose you buy 1 ETH for $2,000 with a $10 fee, then another 1 ETH for $3,000 with a $10 fee. Your recorded acquisition cost is $5,020 for 2 ETH, or $2,510 per ETH.

If you sell 0.5 ETH at $3,200 per ETH with a $5 fee, gross proceeds are $1,600 and net proceeds are $1,595. The sold quantity has a cost basis of $1,255. Realized profit is therefore $340.

You still hold 1.5 ETH with a remaining cost basis of $3,765. At a current price of $3,000 per ETH, that holding is worth $4,500 and its unrealized profit is $735. A later price change affects unrealized profit, not the already recorded sale.

Why adding a wallet cannot reveal every purchase

A transfer into a wallet might be a purchase, a transfer between your own accounts, a reward or a gift. The transfer itself does not reliably disclose its acquisition cost. Orbit keeps wallet holdings separate from recorded-trade holdings so that the same asset is not automatically counted twice.

What to review when P&L looks wrong

  • Missing purchases or sales, especially from an exchange that predates the wallet.
  • Fees recorded in the wrong currency or omitted entirely.
  • Transfers entered as purchases, or the same fills imported twice.
  • A missing or stale valuation, or a different token with the same ticker.
  • An incomplete history window from a chain or exchange provider.
