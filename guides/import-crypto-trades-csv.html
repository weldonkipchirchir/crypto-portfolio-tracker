Orbit / CSV trade import
Orbit product guide · Trade records

Import crypto trades from CSV with costs and fees intact.

Use Orbit’s normalized CSV format to review buy and sell records before adding them to your portfolio. Exchange exports may need their columns converted first.

Download sample CSV

Supported CSV columns

ColumnWhat to enter
dateTrade date and time, preferably ISO 8601 with an explicit UTC offset, such as 2026-01-01T12:00:00Z.
symbolThe ledger asset symbol or alias. Use consistent identities across purchases and sales.
sidebuy or sell.
quantityA positive number of asset units. Use the side column, not a negative quantity, to mark a sale.
priceNonnegative USD price per asset unit, not the total trade value. Enter the actual execution price; do not use zero as a substitute for unknown cost.
feeOptional nonnegative fee in USD for the whole trade. Blank or omitted fees default to zero; that default does not prove the trade had no fee.
accountOptional account label. Blank or omitted labels default to Manual.

Preview before you import

  1. Sign in to Orbit and choose Import CSV.
  2. Select the normalized file and choose Preview import.
  3. Review valid rows, errors, duplicate counts and the sample records.
  4. Correct the file when necessary, preview again, then confirm the import.

A file supports at most 10,000 data rows, subject to the request-size limit. A preview with validation errors cannot be confirmed. Review timezone, quantities, unit prices and fee currency before saving.

What counts as a duplicate?

Orbit compares the normalized date, asset, side, quantity, price, fee and account label with existing records and other rows in the file. Matching records are skipped. Two legitimate fills can share these values, so inspect identical fills rather than assuming every match is accidental. Reimporting an unchanged file that was already saved produces an already-imported error rather than adding it again.

Common import problems

  • Missing columns: include date, symbol, side, quantity and price with those exact headers.
  • Sale exceeds holdings: recover earlier acquisition records, correct dates, or check the asset alias. A wallet balance does not automatically populate recorded-trade holdings.
  • Wrong valuation: use unit price in USD, not a total order value or an unconverted fee paid in a different asset.
  • Transfers in an export: do not turn every deposit or withdrawal into a buy or sell. Review what actually happened.

This format imports portfolio trade records. It is not a universal exchange-export parser, a tax report or proof that your transaction history is complete.
